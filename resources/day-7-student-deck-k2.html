Slide 1 · Title & Promise R2, R6

“Everybody makes mistakes. Even me — yesterday I forgot to do something I said I would. Mistakes do not end our day. We fix it, then we come back.”

Look for

  • Students relax — this lesson lowers anxiety when done well
  • Students volunteer their own mistakes

If it breaks

  • Tell a real, small mistake of your own. That is what makes it safe.

Support adult

  • Do not use a student's real mistake as the example

Language support

  • Fully accessible with a demonstrated story.

Visual: Repair icon. Tell a one-sentence story of a mistake you made this week.

Slide 2 · Regulate R1

“Smell the flower. Blow out the candle. Long and slow. Three times.” Model it with them every single time — do not narrate from the front without doing it.

Look for

  • Exhale longer than inhale
  • Shoulders drop
  • Gesture copied

If it breaks

  • If silliness: model silently once, then invite them back. Do not lecture.

Support adult

  • Breathe audibly beside any student who cannot settle

Language support

  • No language demand — gesture carries it.
  • Same two gestures every day for ten days.

Visual: Flower and candle icons with matching hand gestures.

Slide 3 · Orient R6

“Two kinds. An OOPS is when I forget — I left my chair out. I fix it fast. An OUCH is when I hurt somebody's feelings or body. That one needs more than fast.” Act out one of each.

Look for

  • Students correctly sort examples into oops and ouch
  • Students use the words themselves

If it breaks

  • Give three more examples and let them call out which kind.

Support adult

  • Use these two words consistently all year — they replace adult vocabulary entirely

Language support

  • 'Oops' and 'ouch' are sound-words. They cross languages more easily than abstract labels.

Visual: Pencil for oops, heart for ouch. These two words replace clinical vocabulary at this band.

Slide 4 · Connect R6

“I say a mistake. You point — oops or ouch. Ready. I forgot my pencil. I pushed someone. I left my coat out. I said something mean.” Go slowly.

Look for

  • Correct sorting
  • Students using 'not sure' honestly rather than guessing

If it breaks

  • Slow down. Do one at a time with full think time.

Support adult

  • 'Not sure' is a real answer — never treat it as wrong

Language support

  • Pointing is a complete answer.

Visual: Three pictured options. 'Not sure' is a legitimate answer and must be offered.

Slide 5 · Practice R6

“Three steps. Stop. Say what you did — short. Then do it better. Watch me.” Role-play it with a volunteer, with you playing the person who was hurt.

Look for

  • Short acknowledgments rather than long explanations
  • The 'do it better' step actually happening

If it breaks

  • “That was a long story. Try again — short words.”

Support adult

  • Never force an apology. Coach the repair action instead — the words follow.

Language support

  • The sentence is given exactly. That is the scaffold.

Visual: Three repair-step icons. Role-play with the teacher as the person harmed.

Slide 6 · Commit R2, R5, R6

“Pick what you will do FIRST when you make a mistake today. Point to it.” Plan the recovery before it is needed.

Look for

  • Every student points
  • Students use their chosen move when a real mistake happens

If it breaks

  • Assign one.

Support adult

  • When a real mistake happens, quietly point to their choice

Language support

  • Pointing is sufficient.

Visual: Three pictured recovery moves.

Slide 7 · Transition R1, R4, R5

“How we leave is how we start our learning. Stand. Push in. Turn. Walk. Let's practice.” Do not time this at K–2.

Look for

  • Chairs pushed in unprompted
  • Calm bodies, not rushing

If it breaks

  • “Let's try that again. Back to your spot.” Rebuild clean, no commentary on individuals.

Support adult

  • Stand at the destination, not behind the group

Language support

  • Four icons, four words.
  • No timing pressure — it degrades the routine at this age.

Visual: Four numbered step icons. No stopwatch at this band.
