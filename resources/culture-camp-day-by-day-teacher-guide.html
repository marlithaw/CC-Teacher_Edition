Day-by-Day Teacher Guide

Audience: Teachers implementing Culture Camp

This guide is written in instructional blocks because Culture Camp does not happen in one consecutive hour. The minutes below restart inside each block. The structure is meant to live across the actual school day: arrival, breakfast or independent work, Morning Meeting, academic launches, hallway and shared-space routines, cleanup, dismissal rehearsal, and final exit.

This guide follows the actual Matchbook Culture Camp model:

  • The full culture system is taught on Day 1.
  • The full culture system stays live on Days 2-10.
  • Culture is the number one priority during these ten days.
  • If a routine breaks, adults stop, reset, and repractice before protecting pacing.

All absolute windows below are calibrated to a 7:10 school-day start and a 2:00 dismissal start. If a grade band shifts lunch, recess, or specials slightly, keep the opening anchor, the dismissal anchor, and the sequence of the blocks intact.

Rubric Shorthand

Use these rubric codes when planning, observing, or coaching:

  • R1 Regulation and Self-Management
  • R2 Orientation and Agency
  • R3 Voice and Community
  • R4 Practice and Skill Building
  • R5 Accountability and Leadership
  • R6 Restorative Response

Adult Deployment

  • If two adults are present, use Lead Adult for direct teaching and Support Adult for modeling, outlier support, materials, and live corrections.
  • If only one adult is present, collapse both roles into one lane and shorten the number of spoken directions.
  • Student leaders can begin carrying pieces of the routine on Day 4, but adult accountability remains active all 10 days.

How To Read The Script Tables

  • Block Minute means time inside that specific block, not the whole day.
  • The same culture system stays live every day of Culture Camp, even when the block windows shift slightly by grade band.
  • The daily shift is the emphasis, the amount of adult narration, and the degree of student ownership.
  • Teacher language should stay calm, direct, and repeatable.
  • If you need an extra reset, insert it immediately. Do not wait for a "better time."

Day 2: Listening and Speaking Norms

Adult scaffold posture: strong adult lead with less explaining
Priority reset focus: listening body, sentence frames, and faster response to cues

Day 4: Voice Tone and Early Student Roles

Adult scaffold posture: cue, observe, and give short resets
Priority reset focus: tone, role clarity, and protecting dignity during interaction

Block Block Minute Teacher script and action Students do Rubric

Day 7: Repair Language and Recovery

Adult scaffold posture: coach repair without dropping structure
Priority reset focus: routine errors, relational harm, and fast recovery

Block Block Minute Teacher script and action Students do Rubric
Arrival and First Five 0:00-2:00 At the threshold say, "Today we practice what happens after a mistake. We do not fall apart when a routine breaks." Enter knowing the class will practice recovery, not perfection. R1, R2
Arrival and First Five 2:00-5:00 If an entry mistake happens, say, "That is a routine error. The repair is, 'My bad, I'll fix it.' Show me." Use quick repair language and correct the action. R5, R6
Morning Meeting 0:00-2:00 Regulate first and say, "At Matchbook, repair happens after regulation." Breathe and prepare to talk about mistakes calmly. R1, R6
Morning Meeting 2:00-4:00 Teach the difference: "Routine error: 'My bad, I'll fix it.' Relational harm: 'I hurt you when I __. Next time I will __.'" Listen and distinguish between the two kinds of repair. R2, R6
Morning Meeting 4:00-7:00 Give a short prompt: "Tell your partner one small mistake that can be fixed fast." Share low-stakes examples of routine errors. R3, R6
Morning Meeting 7:00-10:00 Role-play both kinds of repair. Say, "No long speeches. No excuses. Repair, then return." Practice routine repair and relational repair language. R4, R6
Morning Meeting 10:00-13:00 Prompt commitments: "What will you do today if your routine breaks?" Make a commitment tied to recovery or repair. R2, R5, R6
Morning Meeting 13:00-15:00 Close with, "Repair is part of the routine, not outside of it." Transition into learning with the expectation of accountable recovery. R2, R6
First Academic Launch 0:00-2:00 If a student blurts or forgets a material, say, "Routine error. Use the repair and fix it." Use quick repair language and restart the behavior. R5, R6
First Academic Launch 2:00-4:00 If a peer interaction crosses a line, pause and say, "Regulate first. Then repair the harm clearly." Slow down, regulate, and attempt a clearer repair. R1, R6
Shared-Space Coaching 0:00-3:00 In shared spaces, say, "Hallway bumps, lunch frustration, recess conflict: repair fast and return to the routine." Apply repair language outside the classroom. R3, R6
Shared-Space Coaching 3:00-6:00 If exclusion or conflict starts, say, "We are not ignoring it, and we are not losing the whole day to it. Repair, then rejoin." Repair and re-enter the routine with adult support. R3, R5, R6
Closing and Dismissal 0:00-3:00 Debrief: "What kind of repair did we use today? Which one still needs work?" Reflect on recovery and honesty. R2, R6
Closing and Dismissal 3:00-5:00 Dismiss with, "Even exits need repair if they break. We leave strong, even after mistakes." Exit with a reset mindset, not a defeated one. R1, R5, R6

Day 8: Student Leadership Without Loss of Fidelity

Adult scaffold posture: step back, but do not lower the bar
Priority reset focus: student-led ownership, houses and identity layered in without weakening the routine

Block Block Minute Teacher script and action Students do Rubric
Arrival and First Five 0:00-2:00 At the threshold say, "Today leaders carry more of the system. Leadership does not mean louder. It means tighter." Enter with the expectation that students will own more of the routine. R1, R5
Arrival and First Five 2:00-5:00 Invite a student leader to cue the first five while you observe. If it gets loose, say, "Leadership only works if fidelity stays high. Reset it." Follow a peer cue and respond to a reset if needed. R4, R5
Morning Meeting 0:00-2:00 Hand roles to students: Board Reader, Time Keeper, Mic Passer, Practice Leader. Say, "I am not disappearing. I am watching whether the routine survives with your ownership." Take roles and prepare to lead with structure. R2, R5
Morning Meeting 2:00-4:00 Allow the Board Reader to open the values and daily prompt. Add, "House pride and identity matter here, but they never replace discipline." Listen to peer leadership and connect identity to responsibility. R2, R3, R5
Morning Meeting 4:00-7:00 Let student leaders run the share. Intervene only if voice protection or structure breaks. Share, listen, and manage the flow with more student control. R3, R5
Morning Meeting 7:00-10:00 Let the Practice Leader cue the routine move. Coach quietly from the side if needed. Follow peer-led practice and take it seriously. R4, R5
Morning Meeting 10:00-13:00 Prompt commitments through a student leader: "Say what you will carry into the day." State commitments with stronger ownership. R2, R5
Morning Meeting 13:00-15:00 Close with, "Leadership is real if the transition stays clean." Let students help cue the move into learning. Transition with peer leadership and less adult talk. R1, R5
First Academic Launch 0:00-2:00 Ask, "Can our student leaders cue the move into work without losing control?" Observe more than speak. Follow peer cues into academic work. R5
First Academic Launch 2:00-4:00 If leadership becomes excitement without discipline, say, "Identity is not a pass on fidelity. Reset the move." Restart the learning transition with tighter control. R1, R5, R6
Shared-Space Coaching 0:00-3:00 Use student leaders in line or at recall: "Show us how a Matchbook leader holds the hallway." Watch and follow student-led public-space routines. R5
Shared-Space Coaching 3:00-6:00 Name evidence when it works: "That was leadership because it made the group tighter, not louder." Internalize that leadership is measured by stronger routine execution. R5
Closing and Dismissal 0:00-3:00 Debrief: "Did leadership strengthen the culture today, or just change who was talking?" Reflect on whether ownership improved fidelity. R2, R5
Closing and Dismissal 3:00-5:00 Let a student leader cue dismissal while you observe for drift. Exit with student-owned structure. R5

Adult scaffold posture: layer celebration without softening expectations
Priority reset focus: keeping structure high on a high-energy day

Block Block Minute Teacher script and action Students do Rubric
Arrival and First Five 0:00-2:00 At the threshold say, "Today we will talk about recognition, but the routine still comes first. Celebration does not lower the bar." Enter with steady structure even if energy is high. R1, R5
Arrival and First Five 2:00-5:00 If excitement turns into noise, say, "Recognition begins with control. Reset the first five." Re-enter or restart with tighter regulation. R1, R4, R6
Morning Meeting 0:00-2:00 Regulate and say, "High energy still needs structure." Regulate and prepare for a celebratory but disciplined tone. R1

Day 10: Readiness Review and Launch

Adult scaffold posture: observe more, intervene only when needed
Priority reset focus: the routines that are still too dependent on adult narration

Block Block Minute Teacher script and action Students do Rubric
Arrival and First Five 0:00-2:00 Greet briefly and observe. Say only, "Show me Day 10." Use a checklist instead of heavy narration. Enter and begin with near-independent execution. R1, R5
Arrival and First Five 2:00-5:00 If the first five still depends on prompting, say, "This is a readiness reset. Show me the version you can own." Restart with stronger ownership. R4, R5
Morning Meeting 0:00-2:00 Run regulation with the fewest possible words. Watch for whether students settle on the first cue. Regulate with minimal adult support. R1
Morning Meeting 2:00-4:00 Let students carry the values and the opening language as much as possible. Name the values and the purpose of the routine. R2, R5
Morning Meeting 4:00-7:00 Observe the share rather than over-coaching it. Only interrupt if the norms collapse. Share and listen with near-independent discipline. R3, R5
Morning Meeting 7:00-10:00 Let students lead the practice move. Note whether they can self-correct after a first miss. Practice with lighter adult intervention. R4, R5
Morning Meeting 10:00-13:00 Ask for commitments and listen for specificity without rewriting every answer for them. State launch-ready commitments with more agency. R2, R5
Morning Meeting 13:00-15:00 Close with, "Show me whether this routine lives beyond this circle." Observe the transition into learning. Transition into work with minimal help. R1, R5
First Academic Launch 0:00-2:00 Start a timer and watch the move into academic work. Only intervene if the routine truly breaks. Begin work while sustaining the culture without heavy narration. R1, R5
First Academic Launch 2:00-4:00 If a reset is needed, say, "This is feedback, not failure. We are identifying the last weak point before launch." Accept the reset and improve the next attempt. R4, R6
Shared-Space Coaching 0:00-3:00 Observe hallway, lunch, recess recall, and return transitions with a readiness lens. Name only the most important correction. Hold routines across public spaces with lighter support. R3, R5
Shared-Space Coaching 3:00-6:00 If one move still collapses, say, "That is the move we keep reteaching next week." Repractice it once with precision. Repractice the highest-need move without drama. R4, R5
Closing and Dismissal 0:00-3:00 Debrief honestly: "What is launch-ready? What still needs reteach?" Use green, yellow, or red language if needed. Reflect on readiness without pretending everything is finished. R2, R5
Closing and Dismissal 3:00-5:00 Close with recognition and a launch statement: "Culture Camp does not end our routines. It proves we can carry them." Exit understanding that the system continues after Day 10. R2, R5

Leader and Coach Read-Through

When observing a Culture Camp classroom, leaders and coaches should be able to answer these questions with low-inference evidence:

  • Did the teacher teach the full culture system across the day, not just in Morning Meeting?
  • Did block minutes restart naturally in separate parts of the day, or did the plan still behave like one fake continuous lesson?
  • Did the teacher stop to reset and repractice when routines broke?
  • Did student ownership increase over the ten days without lowering fidelity expectations?
  • Did the teacher connect what happened in each block back to the rubric codes?
