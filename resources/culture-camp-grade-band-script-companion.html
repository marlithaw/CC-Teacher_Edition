Three Rules That Override Everything

1 · We correct the routine, not the child
Every reset script in this guide rebuilds the procedure. None of them name or characterize a student.
2 · Body orientation, never eye contact
The required behavior is "body turned to the speaker." Eyes toward the speaker and eyes resting on a calm spot are both named on-slide as fully acceptable. No adult in this building requires sustained eye contact — it is a cultural-responsiveness and disability-access requirement, not a preference.
3 · No breath retention at K–2
K–2 uses extended exhale only ("smell the flower, blow the candle"). Hold phases begin at grade 3.

Grades K–2 — full script

BlockLead adult script, look-fors, and resets
1 · Title & Promise
R2
Point to each word. “These are our three big words. Safe. Respectful. Responsible. Say them with me.” Say each word three times, clapping the syllables.

Look for

  • All voices join by the second repetition
  • Students look at the word you point to

If it breaks

  • If voices are scattered: “Let's find one voice. Watch my hand.” Clap the syllables and go again.

Support adult

  • Stand at the far side of the carpet so students turn their bodies outward, not toward one adult

Language support

  • Cognates: respectful/respetuoso, responsible/responsable. Say both.
  • Add a hand gesture to each word — same gesture every day, all ten days.

Visual Shield icon. Point to each word as the class says it. Add a photo of THIS class here once taken.

2 · Regulate
R1
“Before we learn, we get calm. Smell the flower — breathe in. Blow out the candle — long and slow. Again.” Do it three times with them, out loud.

Look for

  • Shoulders drop
  • Exhale is longer than the inhale
  • Students copy the hand gesture

If it breaks

  • If students get silly: “That's okay. Watch me one more time.” Model silently, then invite them back in.

Support adult

  • Sit beside any student who cannot settle and breathe next to them — do not correct verbally

Language support

  • This slide is fully picturable. No language demand.
  • Gesture carries the meaning — keep the same two gestures all year.

Visual Flower and candle icons. Teacher models with real hand gestures — cupped hand to nose, then blowing.

3 · Orient
R2, R3
“Safe means my hands are gentle — show me gentle hands. Respectful means I let others have a turn. Responsible means I start my job without waiting.” Have them act out each one.

Look for

  • Students physically demonstrate gentle hands
  • Students can point to the matching icon when you name a value

If it breaks

  • If they cannot recall: re-teach one value only. Do not push all three. Come back tomorrow.

Support adult

  • Hold up the printed icon cards as each value is named

Language support

  • Cognates are on the slide — say the Spanish aloud.
  • Act out every definition. Movement is the scaffold.

Visual One icon per value. These three icons must stay identical across all ten days and every shared-space deck.

4 · Connect
R3, R4
“We are going to practice talking AND listening. First, everyone say it with me: I feel happy when recess. Now turn to your partner. Knees to knees.” Rehearse chorally before partner talk.

Look for

  • Knees turned toward partner
  • One person talking at a time
  • Students point to a picture if words are hard

If it breaks

  • If both partners talk at once: “Freeze. Speakers raise your hand. Only speakers talk. Go.”

Support adult

  • Partner any student without a partner — do not let a child sit out

Language support

  • Pointing to a picture is a complete answer. Accept it.
  • Choral rehearsal first means every child has heard the sentence before being asked to produce it.

Visual Three pictured options. Students may point instead of speaking — pointing counts as a full answer at this band.

5 · Practice
R3, R4, R5
“Listening has a body. Shoulders turned to the speaker. Ears ready. Your eyes can look at the speaker, OR your eyes can rest on a calm spot — both are good listening. Show me.”

Look for

  • Shoulders and knees oriented toward the speaker
  • Stillness in the body — this is the actual measure, not eye contact

If it breaks

  • “We correct the routine, not the child. Let's build it again — shoulders first.”

Support adult

  • Model the posture silently beside the group

Language support

  • Fully demonstrable. No language required to succeed.
  • Never require eye contact. For some students it is culturally wrong and for others it costs the attention they need to listen.

Visual Body-orientation figure, ear, resting eyes. The eyes icon shows CLOSED/resting eyes on purpose — both options are equally correct.

6 · Commit
R2, R5
“A promise is one thing we can SEE you do. Pick one. Point to it. Say it: Today I will use kind words.” Have three students say theirs aloud.

Look for

  • Every student points to one option
  • Students can name their choice back when asked later in the day

If it breaks

  • If a student cannot choose: “I'll pick with you. Kind words — that's yours today.” Assign it, do not wait.

Support adult

  • Write names next to choices on chart paper so you can refer back after lunch

Language support

  • Pointing is sufficient.
  • Revisit the same three promises daily — repetition builds the vocabulary.

Visual Three pictured promises. Students choose one and point. Keep the same three all week so the choice becomes automatic.

7 · Transition
R1, R4, R5
“How we leave the meeting is how we start our learning. Four steps. Stand. Push in. Turn. Walk. Let's practice.” Do not time this at K–2.

Look for

  • Chairs pushed in without reminder
  • Bodies calm, not rushing

If it breaks

  • “Let's try that again. Back to your spot.” Rebuild it clean — no commentary on individual children.

Support adult

  • Stand at the destination point, not behind the group

Language support

  • Four icons, four words. Point to each icon as you say it.
  • Do NOT add a stopwatch at this band — timing pressure degrades the routine for young children.

R2

Look for

  • Students track the board
  • At least one student can restate why we practice when it is easy

  • If attention drifts: ask a student to restate the last sentence in their own words before continuing.

Support adult

  • Scan for students who are not tracking and move toward them

Language support

  • Cognates: respectful/respetuoso, responsible/responsable.
  • Post the three words with icons on the wall before Day 1.

2 · Regulate
R1
“In for four. Out for six. The breath OUT is the long one — that is the part that calms you down. Three rounds, starting now.” Count aloud with them.

Look for

  • Exhale visibly longer than inhale
  • Shoulders drop by round two

If it breaks

  • If students are performative or silly: “This one is for you, not for me. Eyes down, one more round.”

Support adult

  • Sit near students who struggle and co-regulate by breathing audibly beside them

Language support

  • Counting is the only language demand — accessible at all proficiency levels.
  • Model, do not just describe.

Visual Lungs and calm-face icons. Consider a 4/6 count visual timer bar.

3 · Orient
R2, R3
“Notice what changed from last year. Safe is not about staying out of trouble — it is about how other people feel around you. Respectful is not agreeing — it is making it safe for someone to disagree with you.”

Look for

  • Students can give an example of disagreeing respectfully
  • Students name the shift from self to others

If it breaks

  • If students give only rule-following answers: “That is about you. Try again — who else is affected?”

Support adult

  • Chart student examples so they can be referenced later in the week

Language support

  • Provide the frame: ‘I can disagree respectfully by ___.’
  • Pair with a partner before whole-group share.

Visual Same three value icons used K–8. Consistency across bands is what makes this one system.

4 · Connect
R3, R4
“Ten seconds of think time — no talking. Pick a word from the bank if it helps. Now turn and talk. Speaker goes first, listener says nothing until they finish. Switch when I say switch.”

Look for

  • Silent think time actually happens
  • Only one voice per pair
  • Listener body turned toward speaker

If it breaks

  • If pairs go off task: “Freeze. Listeners, tell me one thing your partner said.” Accountability restores focus.

Support adult

  • Coach pair spacing; note which students default to silence

Language support

  • Word bank is on the slide — point to it explicitly.
  • Allow a one-word answer. It counts.

5 · Practice
R3, R4, R5
“Listening is a body position and a result. Shoulders turned. Eyes on the speaker OR resting somewhere calm — both are real listening. The test is not where your eyes are. The test is whether you can repeat back what they said.”

Look for

  • Body orientation toward speaker
  • Students can accurately repeat a partner's statement

If it breaks

  • “We correct the routine, not the child. Rebuild it — shoulders first, then we go again.”

Support adult

  • Model the posture beside the group without narrating

Language support

  • Repeating back is itself a language scaffold — it gives students a sentence to reuse.

Visual Body-orientation figure. The eyes icon deliberately shows a resting gaze.

6 · Commit
R2, R5
“A commitment is one visible next step — something anyone in this room could SEE you do. Not a feeling. Fill in both blanks. The second blank is the one that matters: who does it help?”

Look for

  • The 'so that' names another person or the group

If it breaks

  • If a student says ‘be good’: “That is a feeling. What would I SEE you doing?”

Support adult

  • Record commitments; you will reconnect to them after lunch

Language support

  • Word bank covers the first blank. Allow the second blank in a home language.

R1, R4, R5

Look for

If it breaks

  • “Reset. We are doing the first thirty seconds again until it is clean.”

Support adult

  • Watch the slowest students and redirect quietly to the step, not the behavior

Language support

1 · Title & Promise
R2

Look for

  • Students are tracking rather than performing boredom
  • At least one student pushes back — that is engagement, take it seriously

If it breaks

  • If a student says this is pointless: “That is a fair position. Give me ten days of data, then tell me.” Do not argue.

Support adult

  • Scan for the students who check out first — they are the ones to invest in this week

Language support

  • Preview 'run them' as an idiom — it means put into practice.

Visual Shield icon. Strong candidate for replacement with a photo of actual students from this school.

2 · Regulate
R1
“Four in, four hold, four out. Here is why it works: the long exhale triggers your vagus nerve, which physically slows your heart rate. You do not have to want it to work. It works anyway. Three rounds.”

Look for

  • Genuine participation rather than performative compliance
  • Students who resist on day one but participate by day three — that is the win

If it breaks

  • If a student refuses: do not escalate. “Sit quietly and let us work. That is enough.” Refusal is not defiance at this age — it is often self-consciousness.

Support adult

  • Participate visibly yourself. Adolescents read adult non-participation instantly.

Language support

  • Numbers carry the protocol.
  • Pre-teach 'vagus nerve' — or replace with 'the nerve that controls your heart rate.'

Visual Lungs, clock, calm icons. Consider a heart-rate line dropping across the four counts.

3 · Orient
R2, R3
“Read the middle one again. Respect before it is earned. That is not weakness — it is the only way anyone in a new room ever gets a chance. If everyone waits to be respected first, nobody moves.”

Look for

  • Students engage with the 'before it is earned' tension — expect argument
  • Students connect the standard to their own experience of a room that did not work

If it breaks

  • If a student says respect must be earned: “Then who goes first? Somebody has to. That is the whole argument.”

Support adult

  • Chart the counterarguments. Taking them seriously is what earns buy-in at this age.

Language support

  • Pre-teach 'extend' and 'repair' in this context.
  • Provide the frame: ‘I agree/disagree because ___.’

Visual Same three icons used K–8. The visual continuity is deliberate and worth naming to students.

4 · Connect
R3, R4
“Think of a group that actually worked — a team, a class, a job, a friend group. What did people DO? Not how it felt. What did they do. Ninety seconds, speaker first. Listener does not respond until they are done.”

Look for

  • Students name behaviors, not vibes
  • Listeners hold silence — this is the harder skill at this age

If it breaks

  • If pairs stall: give your own example in one sentence, then restart the clock.

Support adult

  • Note which students describe groups OUTSIDE school — that is where the real answer lives

Language support

  • Allow the initial description in a home language, then support translation of the key behavior.
  • Provide: ‘It worked because people ___.’

Visual Two-people icon. Consider projecting a 90-second countdown.

5 · Practice
R3, R4, R5
“We are not going to police your eyes. For some of you, forced eye contact makes it harder to listen, and in some families it is straight-up rude. So the standard is body orientation, and the proof is whether you can restate what the person said. That is measurable. Eyes are not.”

Look for

  • Accurate restatement
  • Body orientation maintained without reminder

If it breaks

  • “We correct the routine, not the person. Reset the orientation and we run it again.”

Support adult

  • Model restating a student's point precisely — they will copy your precision

Language support

  • Restating is the core academic language skill here — it transfers directly to discussion in content classes.

Visual Body-orientation figure with a resting-gaze icon. Naming the no-eye-policing rule explicitly is part of the lesson.
