Slide 1 · Title & Promise R3

“I saw Marcus push in three chairs. I saw Aaliyah help someone find their coat. When we notice, we say it out loud.” Use real names and real actions from today.

Look for

  • Students light up at being named
  • Students start noticing each other

If it breaks

  • Name more examples. Specificity is what makes it work.

Support adult

  • Track who you have named. Every child needs to be named this week.

Language support

  • Being named requires no English to receive.

Visual: Eye icon. Immediately name three things you saw specific children do.

Slide 2 · Regulate R1

“Smell the flower. Blow out the candle. Long and slow. Three times.” Model it with them every single time — do not narrate from the front without doing it.

Look for

  • Exhale longer than inhale
  • Shoulders drop
  • Gesture copied

If it breaks

  • If silliness: model silently once, then invite them back. Do not lecture.

Support adult

  • Breathe audibly beside any student who cannot settle

Language support

  • No language demand — gesture carries it.
  • Same two gestures every day for ten days.

Visual: Flower and candle icons with matching hand gestures.

Slide 3 · Orient R3

“If I say 'you are nice,' do you know what to do again tomorrow? No! But if I say 'you pushed in the chairs,' now you know. Say what you SAW.”

Look for

  • Students produce specific praise
  • Students correct vague praise to specific

If it breaks

  • Give a vague one and ask them to fix it.

Support adult

  • Model specific praise constantly. Your narration teaches this, not the slide.

Language support

  • Provide the exact frame: ‘I saw you ___.’

Visual: Eye vs heart contrast. Demonstrate the difference with two praise statements.

Slide 4 · Connect R3

“Tell your partner one thing you saw them do today. Point to the picture if that helps. Then trade.”

Look for

  • Every child receives one
  • Actions named rather than personality

If it breaks

  • Assign a pairing and prompt with something you saw.

Support adult

  • Make sure nobody is left unnamed. Step in if needed.

Language support

  • Pointing is a complete answer.

Visual: Three pictured actions.

Slide 5 · Practice R3, R5

“Some friends get noticed a lot. Some do not. Today your job is to find someone who does not get noticed much and tell them what you saw.”

Look for

  • Students seek out less-noticed peers
  • Quiet children receive recognition

If it breaks

  • Name the children who have not been noticed and assign partners.

Support adult

  • You must track this. The children who need it most are the easiest to skip.

Language support

  • Developing English speakers are frequently under-recognized. Watch for it specifically.

Visual: Three icons on noticing the unnoticed. This is the K-2 seed of equity in recognition.

Slide 6 · Commit R2, R3

“Pick who you will notice today. Point to it. Then go tell them what you saw.”

Look for

  • Students follow through
  • Less-noticed children receive attention

If it breaks

  • Assign a specific person.

Support adult

  • Check at the end of the day whether it happened

Language support

  • Pointing is sufficient.

Visual: Three pictured options.

Slide 7 · Transition R1, R4, R5

“How we leave is how we start our learning. Stand. Push in. Turn. Walk. Let's practice.” Do not time this at K–2.

Look for

  • Chairs pushed in unprompted
  • Calm bodies, not rushing

If it breaks

  • “Let's try that again. Back to your spot.” Rebuild clean, no commentary on individuals.

Support adult

  • Stand at the destination, not behind the group

Language support

  • Four icons, four words.
  • No timing pressure — it degrades the routine at this age.

Visual: Four numbered step icons. No stopwatch at this band.
