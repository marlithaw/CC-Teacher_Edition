Slide 1 · Title & Promise R2

Show a slumped posture, then an upright one. “What is my body saying? You did not need words, did you? Your body talks too.”

Look for

  • Students read the two postures correctly
  • Students try the postures themselves

If it breaks

  • Re-demonstrate more exaggerated. Young children read big signals.

Support adult

  • Stand where all students can see the full body

Language support

  • Entirely visual — this lesson is fully accessible without English.
  • Let students name the feeling in any language.

Visual: Person icon. Demonstrate two postures and let students guess the feeling.

Slide 2 · Regulate R1

“Smell the flower. Blow out the candle. Long and slow. Three times.” Model it with them every single time — do not narrate from the front without doing it.

Look for

  • Exhale longer than inhale
  • Shoulders drop
  • Gesture copied

If it breaks

  • If silliness: model silently once, then invite them back. Do not lecture.

Support adult

  • Breathe audibly beside any student who cannot settle

Language support

  • No language demand — gesture carries it.
  • Same two gestures every day for ten days.

Visual: Flower and candle icons with matching hand gestures.

Slide 3 · Orient R2, R3

“Ready has three parts. Feet flat. Hands calm. Shoulders turned. Show me feet. Show me hands. Show me shoulders.” Call them one at a time.

Look for

  • Students can produce each part on cue
  • The whole posture assembles without full narration by Friday

If it breaks

  • Re-teach one part only. Do not rebuild all three at once.

Support adult

  • Kneel beside students who need a physical model

Language support

  • Three icons, three body parts — no language required.

Visual: Three body-part icons. Photograph an actual student modeling this and swap it in.

Slide 4 · Connect R3, R4

“Make a body. Your partner guesses. Point to the picture — you do not have to say the word.” Rehearse the sentence chorally first.

Look for

  • Partners take turns without reminder
  • Students point when the word is hard

If it breaks

  • “Freeze. One person poses. One person guesses. Go.”

Support adult

  • Partner anyone left out

Language support

  • Pointing is a full answer.
  • Posing is a language-free way to participate completely.

Visual: Three feeling icons. Partners take turns posing and guessing.

Slide 5 · Practice R3, R4, R5

“Feet. Hands. Eyes — you can look at me OR let your eyes rest somewhere calm. Both are good. Now show me all three at once.”

Look for

  • All three assembled together
  • Stillness — that is the measure, not where eyes point

If it breaks

  • “We correct the routine, not the child. Feet first.”

Support adult

  • Model silently beside the group

Language support

  • Fully demonstrable.
  • Never require eye contact — resting eyes is equally correct here.

Visual: Same three icons as the Orient slide — repetition is deliberate at this band.

Slide 6 · Commit R2, R5

“Pick the one that is hardest for you. Point to it.” Take three volunteers aloud, then move on.

Look for

  • Every student points
  • Students pick honestly rather than picking the easiest

If it breaks

  • Assign one. Do not wait for a choice.

Support adult

  • Chart names beside choices

Language support

  • Pointing is sufficient.

Visual: Three pictured promises matching the body parts taught.

Slide 7 · Transition R1, R4, R5

“How we leave is how we start our learning. Stand. Push in. Turn. Walk. Let's practice.” Do not time this at K–2.

Look for

  • Chairs pushed in unprompted
  • Calm bodies, not rushing

If it breaks

  • “Let's try that again. Back to your spot.” Rebuild clean, no commentary on individuals.

Support adult

  • Stand at the destination, not behind the group

Language support

  • Four icons, four words.
  • No timing pressure — it degrades the routine at this age.

Visual: Four numbered step icons. No stopwatch at this band.
