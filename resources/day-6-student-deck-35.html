Slide 1 · Title & Promise R2

“Nobody is sent to use a tool. You choose one before you need it badly. The whole point is getting ahead of it instead of cleaning up after it.”

Look for

  • Students separate tools from consequences
  • Students name a moment they wish they had used one

If it breaks

  • Name a time you regulated yourself as an adult. Specifically.

Support adult

  • Tools must be physically accessible without asking permission, or students will not use them

Language support

  • Pre-teach 'regulation' with a demonstration before the definition.

Visual: Shield icon. Display the tool menu students will choose from.

Slide 2 · Regulate R1

“In for four, out for six. The breath OUT is the one that calms you — that is the whole trick. Three rounds.” Count aloud with them.

Look for

  • Exhale visibly longer
  • Shoulders drop by round two

If it breaks

  • “This one is for you, not for me. Eyes down, one more round.”

Support adult

  • Co-regulate beside students who struggle

Language support

  • Counting is the only demand.
  • Model rather than describe.

Visual: Lungs and calm-face icons; optional 4/6 count bar.

Slide 3 · Orient R1, R2

“Three tools. Try all three this week, then keep the one that actually works for you. They do not work the same for everyone and that is fine.”

Look for

  • Students trying all three rather than defaulting to one
  • Students articulating which works for them

If it breaks

  • Practice one tool per day rather than all three at once if it is overwhelming.

Support adult

  • Model using one yourself during a genuinely stressful moment

Language support

  • The 5-4-3-2-1 tool has high vocabulary load — pair with pictures of the five senses.

Visual: Three tool icons. Print as a menu card taped inside each desk.

Slide 4 · Connect R2, R4

“Name the moment your body is hardest to manage, and why. The 'because' matters — it tells you what the tool needs to do.”

Look for

  • Specific moments rather than 'all day'
  • The 'because' names a real cause

If it breaks

  • Offer your own hardest moment first.

Support adult

  • Track which moments students name — that is your schedule data

Language support

  • Word bank covers the first blank; allow the 'because' in a home language.

Visual: Two-people icon with word bank posted.

Slide 5 · Practice R1, R4

“Regulate, move, engage. In that order. When transitions fall apart it is almost always because we skipped the first step and went straight to moving.”

Look for

  • Tool used before standing
  • Cleaner transition than yesterday

If it breaks

  • “Reset. Sit down. We are doing the regulate step first.”

Support adult

  • Cue the first step verbally until it becomes automatic

Language support

  • Three icons carry the sequence.

Visual: Three sequential icons. Emphasize that the order is the whole point.

Slide 6 · Commit R2, R5

“Name the tool and name the moment. Before, not after. That is the difference between a tool and a repair.”

Look for

  • A specific moment named
  • 'Before' rather than 'when I get upset'

If it breaks

  • “When exactly? Name the time on the schedule.”

Support adult

  • Record; notice aloud when you see it used

Language support

  • Word bank covers the tool.

Visual: Check icon.

Slide 7 · Transition R1, R4, R5

“How we leave the meeting is how we enter the learning. We time it as one class, against our own record — not against each other.”

Look for

  • Chairs in unprompted
  • No pushing or racing

If it breaks

  • “Reset. First thirty seconds again until it is clean.”

Support adult

  • Redirect to the step, not the behavior

Language support

  • Icons carry the sequence.
  • Post the class time — numbers are accessible.

Visual: Four step icons plus a clock showing the class's previous time.
