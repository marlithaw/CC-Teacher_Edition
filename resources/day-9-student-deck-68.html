Why Most Praise Fails

Trait, Action, And Reach

Trait praise
“You're a good kid.” Collapses the first time you fail.
Action praise
“You restated her point.” Repeatable and checkable.
Reach

Why this worksTrait praise ties worth to outcomes, so failure becomes identity. Action praise gives you a move that survives a bad day.

Slide 1 · Title & Promise R3, R5

“Recognition is not random and it is not neutral. In every school, the same kinds of students get named over and over. If you tracked it here for a month you would see a pattern, and the pattern would tell you who this building is actually built for.”

Look for

  • Students engage analytically rather than defensively
  • Students name patterns they have already noticed

If it breaks

  • If it becomes an attack on staff: “This is a pattern, not a villain. Patterns are fixable.”

Support adult

  • Be prepared for students to name real inequities. They are usually right.

Language support

  • Pre-teach 'distribution' and 'pattern' in this analytical sense.

Visual: Eye icon. Consider a simple distribution chart of who gets recognized.

Slide 2 · Regulate R1

“Four in, four hold, four out. The long exhale slows your heart rate through the vagus nerve. You do not have to believe in it. Three rounds.”

Look for

  • Genuine rather than performative participation
  • Resisters who join by day three — that is the win

If it breaks

  • If a student refuses: “Sit quietly and let us work.” Refusal here is usually self-consciousness, not defiance.

Support adult

  • Participate visibly. Adolescents read adult non-participation instantly.

Language support

  • Numbers carry it.
  • Pre-teach 'vagus nerve' or say 'the nerve that controls heart rate.'

Visual: Lungs, clock, calm icons; optional heart-rate line dropping.

Slide 3 · Orient R3

“Trait praise ties your worth to results, so the first time you fail it becomes evidence against you. Action praise gives you a move. And then the question almost nobody asks: who is getting any of it?”

Look for

  • Students connect trait praise to their own fear of failing
  • Students raise the distribution question themselves

If it breaks

  • Ask: when did praise ever make you feel worse? Usually it was trait praise before a failure.

Support adult

  • Audit your own distribution honestly before teaching this or students will smell it

Language support

  • Pre-teach 'trait' vs 'action.'

Visual: Three icons: trait, action, and distribution.

Slide 4 · Connect R3, R5

“Who gets named around here, and who never does? And what do those two groups have in common? Be honest. I am not going to get defensive — if the pattern is real, I want to know it.”

Look for

  • Honest pattern identification
  • Students naming quiet, struggling, or newly arrived peers

If it breaks

  • If students soften it to protect you: “Say the real answer. I can take it.”

Support adult

  • Do not defend. Write down what they say. Then act on it — they will check.

Language support

  • Newly arrived and developing English speakers are almost always in the missed group. Let them say so.

Visual: Two-people icon.

Slide 5 · Practice R3, R5

“Name the action so specifically they could repeat it. Check your own reach — who have you not recognized in two weeks? And go direct. Public recognition is great for some people and genuinely awful for others. Ask, or go quiet and one-on-one.”

Look for

  • Specific action-based recognition
  • Students recognizing outside their friend group
  • Awareness that public praise is not universally welcome

If it breaks

  • “That was about them, not to them. Go say it.”

Support adult

  • Some students find public recognition punishing. Never force it.

Language support

  • Direct one-on-one recognition is far more accessible than a public callout for a developing speaker.

Visual: Three icons on deliberate recognition practice.

Slide 6 · Commit R2, R3

“Somebody outside your circle. Name the specific action. Say it directly to them this week. It costs you nothing and it is one of the few things that reliably changes how someone experiences this building.”

Look for

  • A peer outside the usual circle named
  • Follow-through within the week

If it breaks

  • “Who have you not spoken to this month? Start there.”

Support adult

  • Follow up. The follow-up is what makes it real.

Language support

  • Provide: ‘I noticed you ___. That mattered because ___.’

Visual: Check icon.

Slide 7 · Transition R1, R4, R5

“How we leave is how we enter. A clean exit means the next class starts on time instead of burning ninety seconds. That is time you get back.”

Look for

  • Chairs in unprompted
  • Collective timing, no racing

If it breaks

  • “Reset. First thirty seconds again, clean.”

Support adult

  • Position yourself at the destination

Language support

  • Icons carry the sequence.
  • Post the time.

Visual: Four step icons plus a clock with the class's previous time.
