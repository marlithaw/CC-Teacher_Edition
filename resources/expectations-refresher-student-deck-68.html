The Four Checkpoints

What We’re Resetting

Dress code
In standard before the bell.
Phones
Away and silent, out of sight.
Backpacks
Stowed, not on desks or aisles.
Hall passes
Out and visible, one at a time.
Why this worksNone of these are about control for its own sake. Phones fragment attention, blocked aisles are a safety issue, and visible passes keep the building accountable and safe. The rule follows the reason.

Slide 1 · Title & Promise R1, R5

“Every system drifts — phones creep out, passes get skipped, backpacks pile up. We reset the agreements we made, on purpose, together.”

Look for

  • Students engage without defensiveness
  • Reset read as shared, not punitive

If it breaks

  • If cynical: “Agreements you never revisit stop being yours. This keeps them yours.”

Support adult

  • Be transparent about which expectations slipped and why they exist.

Language support

  • Non-verbal signals reduce language load in transitions.

Visual: Reset icon. Honest, not punitive, framing.

Slide 2 · Regulate R1

“Box breath before we reset — four, four, four. This lets you take the standard as information instead of a callout.”

Look for

  • Controlled exhale
  • Lower defensiveness

If it breaks

  • Do it with them.

Support adult

  • Breathe alongside anyone tense.

Language support

  • Gesture carries the count.

Visual: Three breath icons, 4-4-4.

Slide 3 · Orient R1, R5

“Four checkpoints — dress code, phones, backpacks, hall passes. For each one, name the reason, not just the rule.”

Look for

  • Students can state the reason behind each
  • All four checkpoints recalled

If it breaks

  • Re-teach the checkpoint with the weakest rationale.

Support adult

  • Post the four; tie each to its actual reason.

Language support

  • Four labeled icons anchor the checklist.

Visual: Four checkpoint icons. Reason before rule.

Slide 4 · Connect R2

“With your partner: which checkpoint slipped most, and what is the honest reason it is easy to let go?”

Look for

  • Candid, non-defensive analysis
  • Restating before responding

If it breaks

  • Frame: “___ slips because ___; the fix is ___.”

Support adult

  • Reward honesty; do not turn shares into confessions.

Language support

  • Sentence frame supports the response.

Visual: Two-person talk icon. Ninety seconds, then share.

Slide 5 · Practice R4, R5

“Rehearse the standard: phones away, aisles clear, one visible pass. We run it until it is automatic again.”

Look for

  • Phones out of sight
  • Clear aisles; pass system followed

If it breaks

  • Reset and re-run the checkpoint.

Support adult

  • Rehearse the checkpoint the class named as loosest.

Language support

  • Physical/visible standard is accessible to all.

Visual: Three icons for the reset checkpoints.

Slide 6 · Commit R2, R5

“One commitment: name the checkpoint, the reason behind it, and who it protects. Make it observable.”

Look for

  • Commitment tied to a real reason
  • Observable action

If it breaks

  • Push vague ones to a visible behavior.

Support adult

  • Log commitments for Friday recognition.

Language support

  • Frame supplies structure.

Visual: Check icon.

Slide 7 · Transition R1, R4, R5

“This reset only means something if it holds in every room and when no one is watching. That is what makes it yours.”

Look for

  • Students extend the reset beyond this room
  • Ownership language, not compliance language

If it breaks

  • Re-anchor on the four checkpoints and their reasons.

Support adult

  • Reinforce the reset across the day, not just here.

Language support

  • Four icons, four words.

Visual: Four icons. Carry the standard building-wide.
