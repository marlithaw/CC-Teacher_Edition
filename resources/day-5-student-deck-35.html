Slide 1 · Title & Promise R2

“Here is why commitments break. When it is only to yourself, dropping it costs nothing. The second someone else is counting on it, it changes. Today every commitment gets a name attached.”

Look for

  • Students recognize the self-only pattern
  • Students engage with the idea of naming a beneficiary

If it breaks

  • Ask for a show of hands: who has dropped a promise to themselves this week? Normalize it.

Support adult

  • Have the week's chart present

Language support

  • Pre-teach 'counting on' as an idiom.

Visual: Handshake icon. Have the week's commitment chart visible.

Slide 2 · Regulate R1

“In for four, out for six. The breath OUT is the one that calms you — that is the whole trick. Three rounds.” Count aloud with them.

Look for

  • Exhale visibly longer
  • Shoulders drop by round two

If it breaks

  • “This one is for you, not for me. Eyes down, one more round.”

Support adult

  • Co-regulate beside students who struggle

Language support

  • Counting is the only demand.
  • Model rather than describe.

Visual: Lungs and calm-face icons; optional 4/6 count bar.

Slide 3 · Orient R2, R3

“Three tests. Visible — could someone see it? Timed — when exactly? Owed — who else gets something from it? A commitment that fails any of these is a wish.”

Look for

  • Students testing their own commitments against all three
  • Students catching 'someday' language in each other

If it breaks

  • Take a weak commitment and fix it aloud with the class using the three tests.

Support adult

  • Chart the three tests where students can point to them

Language support

  • Provide: ‘Someone would see me ___ at ___ and it helps ___.’

Visual: Three criteria icons. Post permanently — students will test commitments against these.

Slide 4 · Connect R3, R4

“Share your commitment. Your partner runs all three tests on it — visible, timed, owed. If it fails one, help them fix it. Then switch.”

Look for

  • Partners genuinely testing rather than agreeing
  • Commitments getting revised in real time

If it breaks

  • “Which test did it fail? Fix that one.”

Support adult

  • Circulate and listen for commitments that pass all three — read those aloud

Language support

  • Word bank supplies the beneficiary.

Visual: Two-people icon with word bank.

Slide 5 · Practice R4, R5, R6

“Everyone drops commitments. That is not the failure. The failure is pretending you did not, or making a long speech about it. Notice. Name it in one sentence. Restart. That is the whole recovery.”

Look for

  • Students self-catching before adult correction
  • One-sentence acknowledgments rather than explanations

If it breaks

  • “That was an excuse, not a name. Try again in one sentence.”

Support adult

  • Model this yourself when you drop something — students copy what adults model here

Language support

  • Provide the exact sentence: ‘I said I would ___ and I didn't. Restarting now.’

Visual: Three recovery icons. This directly sets up Day 7.

Slide 6 · Commit R2, R5

“All three blanks. Visible action, specific time, named person. Run the three tests on your own before you write it down.”

Look for

  • All three tests satisfied
  • A real person named in the third blank

If it breaks

  • “Which test does it fail?”

Support adult

  • Record; reconnect after lunch

Language support

  • Word bank covers the beneficiary.

Visual: Check icon. Must pass all three tests.

Slide 7 · Transition R1, R4, R5

“How we leave the meeting is how we enter the learning. We time it as one class, against our own record — not against each other.”

Look for

  • Chairs in unprompted
  • No pushing or racing

If it breaks

  • “Reset. First thirty seconds again until it is clean.”

Support adult

  • Redirect to the step, not the behavior

Language support

  • Icons carry the sequence.
  • Post the class time — numbers are accessible.

Visual: Four step icons plus a clock showing the class's previous time.
